Chapter 3: Designing a scroll wheel

Some quick and dirty CAD modeling

1.1 3D-printed circuit boards

With this working sensor pad in hand, I wanted to progress my design into a scroll wheel, inspired by the early iPods. Those let you scroll through your music library by moving your finger around a touch sensitive wheel.
Since I own a 3D-printer, of course i wanted to use it. After all, if all you have is a 3D-printer, every problem looks like a nail! I was fairly confident this would work because I'd seen makers use 3D-printers to create home-brewn circuit boards.

1.2 CAD Design

So I opened Fusion 360 and started designing my scroll wheel.
The touch wheel has five sensor pads, separated by 1mm wide squiggly line-gaps. I also designed through-holes so I could solder my solid-core wire to the pads on the front and route the wires through to the back. To keep everything tidy, I added channels for the wire aong the back side of the part.
Finally, I designed a front cover so the copper sensor pads are not touched directly and are somewhat protected.

1.3 Assembly

After 3D-printing both parts, I applied copper tape to the front. This went very smooth because i could use the edges of my 3D-printed part as a cutting guide for the tape.

However, I messed up the through holes a little. To be honest, I had to redesign and print the parts a scond time, because in the first version tolerances were to tight. For the second itearation, I enlarged the throughholes without giving it much thought. This left very little material between the through-holes and the edges of the sensor pads, causing the copper tape to tear in those spots.
But since I wanted to see quick results, I carried on anyway, telling myself that I’d fix it in the next iteration (we’ll see if that actually happens ;)).

But the mistakes didn’t stop there. My string of errors continued with the cover. Because the copper tape had torn, I had to bend the wires a little to get them soldered in place. As a result, they now ended up in slightly different spots than originally planned. Unfortunately, that meant the cutouts in the cover, which I had thoughtfully planned in advance, no longer lined up with my soldering joints.

Finally, since the tollerances of the channels for my wiring were also off, I had to design a last part: A spacer ring that I glued to the underside of my sensor wheel. This gave me a flat surface that could be taped down to my desk with double-sided tape.
