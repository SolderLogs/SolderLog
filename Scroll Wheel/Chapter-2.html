Chapter 1: Start the testing

How to set up a basic sensor and basic code

1.1 How It Started

To get things started, I glued a piece of copper tape to a 3d-printed square and soldered a wire to it. I then connected the wire to D0 and a LED in series with a resistor to D1. Next, I fired up the Arduino IDE to write my first simple test sketch. This sketch reads the touch value reported by the ESP and prints it to the serial monitor.

1.2 Capacitance and Formulas

Before I started tinkering, I wanted to understand how capacitive sensing can detect when a sensor is touched. In a nutshell, a finger influences the electric field around a sensor. When a finger approaches a sensor, it changes the effective capacitance of the sensor. This change can be measured. The relationship between current, voltage, and capacitance is described by:

$$I(t) = C \frac{dV(t)}{dt}$$

For a constant capacitance, this can be rearranged to:

$$C = \frac{I(t)}{\frac{dV(t)}{dt}}$$

This allows us to derive capacitance from the instantaneous current \(I(t)\) divided by the rate of change of voltage \(\frac{dV(t)}{dt}\) across the capacitor or sensor. However, since I am using an ESP32-S3, I can take advantage of its integrated capacitive touch hardware . Pins D0 to D5 and D8 to D12 can be used for touch sensing. To understand what the ESP does, the formula above needs to be rearranged again:

$$\frac{dV(t)}{dt} = \frac{I(t)}{C}$$

Under the assumption of constant current \(I\) and capacitance \(C\) over the measured interval, the voltage change can be expressed as follows:

$$\Delta V = \frac{I}{C} \Delta t$$

Rearranging this for time gives:

$$\Delta t = \frac{C \Delta V}{I}$$

1.3 Conclusion

So, under the assumption of a constant current \(I\) and a fixed voltage change \(\Delta V\), a smaller capacitance charges faster than a larger one.

  1. When we connect a piece of copper tape to a touch-enabled GPIO pin, such as D0 on the XIAO ESP32-S3, the electrode forms an electrical capacitive system with its surroundings.
  2. The touch sensing hardware of the ESP repeatedly charges and discharges this capacitor.
  3. When a finger approaches the copper tape, it changes the effective capacitance. This leads to a change in the length of the charge and discharge cycle.
  4. The touch hardware measures the time needed for a fixed number of charge-discharge cycles and returns this time as raw data. By monitoring changes in this value, it is possible to detect when a finger approaches or touches the sensor.
